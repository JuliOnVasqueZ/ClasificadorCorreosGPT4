Financial analysts have said that not allowing Sierra Pacific's two utilities to sell their assets would have a ripple effect on the company's financial position. The asset sales are tied to low-priced power contracts, and if the sales are canceled, the low-priced power will be gone as well. "If the divestiture is stopped...the state may be dealing with a much bigger rate increase," Steve Fleishman, Merrill Lynch utility analyst, told Dow Jones Newswires recently.
