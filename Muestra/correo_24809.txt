To satisfy interest groups, the markets were designed in an awkward way, which soured some deregulation experts on California before the first electron went on the auction block. Among the quirks: The state required utilities to buy nearly all their power on daily spot markets, rather than arranging long-term contracts that might have allowed them to hedge risk. Consumer prices were also fixed, making it impossible for utilities to pass on higher wholesale costs. Paul L.
