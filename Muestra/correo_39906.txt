It also would allow the state water resources department to be the utilities' wholesale power supplier under long-term contracts at least through 2002. The measure drew a protest from CPUC Commissioner Richard Bilas, who argued that the state agency be "removed from that role as quickly as possible." The bill, which was first passed by the state Assembly, was returned to assembly committees last night to iron out changes made in the senate. It then would have to be ratified again by the Assembly.
