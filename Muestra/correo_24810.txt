In New England and the the Middle Atlantic states, as well in as Britain, Chile and Argentina, all places that have restructured electricity markets, regulators have had to adjust market rules to correct flaws. They have found ways to check the tendency of power sellers to exploit infant markets and charge high prices, Mr. Joskow said. Regulators have also had to establish new markets that, through price signals, encourage power companies to build enough generating capacity so that they have reserves for peak hours.
