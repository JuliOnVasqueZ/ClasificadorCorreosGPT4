It lists available power "products" and the asking price for both buyers and sellers. Lewis and other power marketers monitor City Light's projected power needs and then use the phone or Internet to schedule electricity for the next hour, next day or months ahead. They try to find the best price available, but it's clearly a sellers' market. Utilities routinely buy power that used to cost $30 per megawatt hour for more than $200.
